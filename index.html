NIL 101

What is NIL, in plain words?

NIL stands for Name, Image, and Likeness. It means an athlete can be paid for being themselves — a sponsored post, a local ad, a camp appearance, an autograph — while keeping the right to play. In the states that allow it.

The catch: some states still don't allow high school NIL at all, and the ones that do each have their own rules — with schools adding more on top. A deal that looks great at 16 can cause real problems at 17: with eligibility (whether you're still allowed to play), with taxes, with money that is already spent. That gap is what we cover — and it's why Level 1 comes first.

Name

Your name, printed on anything — a product, an ad, a jersey, an event.

Image

Your photo or video, used to promote something.

Likeness

Anything that clearly means you — an avatar, a drawing, a nickname, a number.
